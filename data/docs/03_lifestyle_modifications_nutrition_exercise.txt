# Document Title: Evidence-Based Lifestyle Interventions: Nutrition, Physical Activity, and Weight Reduction
# Source: American Diabetes Association (ADA) Standards of Care & CDC National Diabetes Prevention Program (NDPP)
# Document ID: DOC-03-LIFESTYLE-NUTRITION

1. DIETARY PATTERNS AND MEDICAL NUTRITION THERAPY (MNT)
Lifestyle modification is the cornerstone of type 2 diabetes management and prevention:
- Macronutrient Distribution: There is no ideal one-size-fits-all percentage of calories from carbohydrates, protein, and fat. Evidence strongly favors healthy dietary patterns such as the Mediterranean Diet, Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension (DASH), and whole-food plant-predominant dietary patterns.
- Dietary Fiber: High intake of dietary soluble and insoluble fiber (minimum 25-35 grams daily or 14g/1000 kcal) delays carbohydrate absorption, blunts postprandial glucose spikes, and promotes healthy gut microbiota. Sources include legumes, whole oats, barley, chia seeds, vegetables, and berries.
- Carbohydrate Quality: Emphasize complex carbohydrates with a low glycemic index and low glycemic load. Strongly discourage sugar-sweetened beverages (soda, fruit juices, sweetened teas), refined grains, pastries, and confectionery products.
- Sodium Restriction: For patients with diabetes and hypertension, limit sodium intake to < 2,300 mg/day (or < 1,500 mg/day in individuals with severe hypertension or chronic kidney disease).
- Healthy Fats: Replace saturated and trans-fats with mono- and polyunsaturated fatty acids found in extra virgin olive oil, nuts, seeds, avocados, and omega-3 fatty acids from wild-caught cold-water fish.

2. PHYSICAL ACTIVITY RECOMMENDATIONS
- Aerobic Exercise: Adults with prediabetes or diabetes should engage in at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic physical activity per week (such as brisk walking, swimming, cycling, or water aerobics) spread over at least 3 days per week, with no more than 2 consecutive days without activity.
- Resistance Training: Moderate-to-vigorous resistance training (free weights, resistance machines, or bodyweight exercises) at least 2 to 3 sessions per week on non-consecutive days enhances muscle insulin sensitivity and glucose uptake via GLUT-4 translocation independent of insulin.
- Interrupting Sedentary Time: Prolonged sitting should be interrupted every 30 minutes with light walking or standing stretches to improve glycemic control.

3. TARGET WEIGHT LOSS
In individuals with overweight or obesity and prediabetes or type 2 diabetes, modest sustained weight loss of 5% to 7% of initial body weight significantly improves glycemic markers, reduces required medication dosages, and reduces progression from prediabetes to diabetes by up to 58% (71% in adults aged >= 60 years) based on the Landmark Diabetes Prevention Program (DPP) trial.
