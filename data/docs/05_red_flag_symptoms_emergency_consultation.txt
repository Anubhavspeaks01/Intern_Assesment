# Document Title: Acute Complications, Red-Flag Symptoms, and Emergency Consultation Guidelines
# Source: Emergency Medicine / Endocrine Society & CDC Public Health Advisories
# Document ID: DOC-05-RED-FLAGS-EMERGENCY

1. IMMEDIATE EMERGENCY RED-FLAG CONDITIONS
Patients with diabetes, metabolic syndrome, or severe hypertension who experience the following acute red-flag symptoms must seek immediate emergency medical care (call emergency medical services / 911 immediately):

2. DIABETIC KETOACIDOSIS (DKA)
DKA is a life-threatening acute metabolic decompensation characterized by profound insulin deficiency, severe hyperglycemia (typically > 250 mg/dL), metabolic acidosis, and ketonemia. While most prevalent in Type 1 diabetes, it can occur in Type 2 diabetes during severe physiologic stress (e.g., sepsis, myocardial infarction, acute pancreatitis).
Red-Flag Warning Signs:
- Fruity, acetone-scented breath odor.
- Nausea, persistent vomiting, and acute abdominal pain.
- Kussmaul respirations: Deep, rapid, labored breathing as respiratory compensation for metabolic acidosis.
- Altered mental status: Progressive drowsiness, confusion, stupor, or coma.
- High levels of ketones detected in urine or blood.

3. HYPEROSMOLAR HYPERGLYCEMIC STATE (HHS)
HHS is an acute metabolic emergency seen primarily in older adults with Type 2 diabetes. It carries a mortality rate of up to 10-20%.
Red-Flag Warning Signs:
- Extreme hyperglycemia: Blood glucose frequently exceeds 600 mg/dL (33.3 mmol/L).
- Profound dehydration, dry mucous membranes, sunken eyes, hypovolemia, and tachycardia.
- Severe neurological impairment: Confusion, delirium, visual hallucinations, hemiparesis, focal seizures, and coma. Ketosis is minimal or absent.

4. SEVERE HYPOGLYCEMIA (BLOOD GLUCOSE < 54 MG/DL)
Severe hypoglycemia is an acute clinical emergency where the patient requires third-party assistance to recover:
- Confusion, bizarre behavior, disorientation, or inability to swallow.
- Generalized seizures, convulsions, or loss of consciousness / diabetic coma.
- Treatment Protocol: Do not force oral fluids into an unconscious patient. Administer emergency intramuscular or nasal glucagon, call 911, and position patient in recovery position to prevent aspiration.

5. HYPERTENSIVE CRISIS AND ACUTE VASCULAR EMERGENCIES
- Severe Blood Pressure Elevation: Systolic BP > 180 mmHg or Diastolic BP > 120 mmHg.
- If accompanied by crushing chest pain, radiating left arm pain (acute coronary syndrome), shortness of breath (acute pulmonary edema), severe sudden headache, or acute focal neurological deficits (stroke warning signs - FAST: Face drooping, Arm weakness, Slurred speech, Time to call 911), immediately transport to emergency department.
