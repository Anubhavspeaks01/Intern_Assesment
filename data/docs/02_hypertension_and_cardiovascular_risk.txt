# Document Title: Hypertension Guidelines, Blood Pressure Staging, and Cardiovascular Complications
# Source: American Heart Association (AHA) / American College of Cardiology (ACC) & CDC
# Document ID: DOC-02-HTN-CARDIO

1. BLOOD PRESSURE CLASSIFICATION CATEGORIES (ACC/AHA GUIDELINES)
Blood pressure is categorized into four clinical tiers based on seated resting office measurements:
- Normal Blood Pressure: Systolic BP < 120 mmHg AND Diastolic BP < 80 mmHg.
- Elevated Blood Pressure: Systolic BP 120-129 mmHg AND Diastolic BP < 80 mmHg.
- Stage 1 Hypertension: Systolic BP 130-139 mmHg OR Diastolic BP 80-89 mmHg.
- Stage 2 Hypertension: Systolic BP >= 140 mmHg OR Diastolic BP >= 90 mmHg.
- Hypertensive Crisis: Systolic BP > 180 mmHg and/or Diastolic BP > 120 mmHg. Requires prompt clinical assessment or emergency hospitalization if accompanied by target organ damage.

2. SYNERGISTIC CARDIOVASCULAR RISK IN DIABETIC PATIENTS
Hypertension and diabetes mellitus frequently coexist, creating an additive, multiplicative risk for macrovascular and microvascular pathology:
- Atherosclerotic Cardiovascular Disease (ASCVD): The leading cause of morbidity and mortality among individuals with diabetes. Patients with both diabetes and hypertension have a 2- to 4-fold higher risk of myocardial infarction, heart failure, and stroke compared to non-diabetic individuals.
- Endothelial Dysfunction: Insulin resistance and elevated glucose levels stimulate vascular smooth muscle cell proliferation, decrease nitric oxide bioavailability, and accelerate arterial stiffness.
- Microvascular Renal Damage (Diabetic Kidney Disease / Nephropathy): Glomerular hypertension combined with hyperglycemia causes progressive glomerulosclerosis, leading to microalbuminuria, macroalbuminuria, and progressive loss of Estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate (eGFR).

3. BLOOD PRESSURE TARGETS IN METABOLIC SYNDROME
For adults with confirmed diabetes and hypertension, clinical guidelines recommend an on-treatment blood pressure target of < 130/80 mmHg, provided it can be safely attained without orthostatic hypotension or adverse drug events. First-line antihypertensive agents with renal-protective properties in diabetic patients include Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme (ACE) inhibitors or Angiotensin Receptor Blockers (ARBs), especially in the presence of urinary albuminuria.
